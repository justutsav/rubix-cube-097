# Section 3 explainer — "How the solution works"

A 57-second animated walkthrough of Speaker 3's section, cut to the **real narration**
from the shot video and built on the **real flowchart** and **real app screens**.

    npm install
    ./scripts-sync-assets.sh     # copies assets into public/ and regenerates src/screens.ts
    npm run render               # -> out/explainer.mp4
    npm run studio               # scrub/edit interactively

## What it is synced to

The narration is lifted straight out of `WhatsApp Video 2026-09-29 at 5.51.14 AM.mp4`,
**95.5s → 152.9s** — the whole of Speaker 3, ending one frame before Speaker 4 starts.
`src/timeline.ts` carries the caption transcript read off that cut's burned-in captions,
one second at a time, so every scene boundary and every sound cue is pinned to a real
timestamp in the tape. The on-screen `SPEAKER 3 · m:ss.s` stamp is that same clock, so any
beat in this video can be checked against the source.

Where the delivered line differs from `docs/Utsav/video/26097_Video_Script_source.md`,
**the frame wins** — this is cut to what was actually said, not to the written script.

## Structure

Each beat opens on the flowchart full-frame with the step named, then the chart shrinks
into the left rail and the explanation takes the right — then back to the chart for the
next step. Eight beats: who she is, no-forms/voice, the interview, the eligibility check,
NSQF mapping, the plan she is handed, the three doors, and the close.

## Where the content comes from

| On screen | Source |
|---|---|
| Flowchart | `assets/flowchart/flowchart.png` — the chart Utsav drew, unmodified |
| Narration | `assets/narration_s3.wav`, cut from the shot video |
| Kiosk + plan phone screens | `assets/screens/*.png`, captured off the running build |
| Plumber (General) · Level 3 · 2 महीने · गैप line | the real `recommend()` output in those screenshots |
| Sound effects | `assets/sfx/*.wav`, generated by `assets/sfx/generate_sfx.py` |

## Sound

Every cue in `timeline.ts` is placed on the second where the **hit** should be heard.
`LEAD` holds each file's measured attack time and `cueStart()` starts the file that much
earlier, so the slow swooshes (0.2-0.3s of attack) stop reading as late.

The sliding cut has its own list, `SLIDE_CUES`: the shot's `transition`/`whoosh_big`
boundary markers come out, and one `paper_slide` — a soft paper-fold swoosh — rides the
middle of each camera move.

    npm run render:slide                                          # with narration
    npx remotion render src/index.ts ExplainerSlide \
      out/explainer_slide_sfxonly.mp4 --props='{"muteNarration":true}'   # sfx only

**Nothing on screen is a mockup of a feature that does not exist.** The IVR and WhatsApp
handsets are drawn UI (we cannot screen-record a phone call or a WhatsApp thread into a
deterministic render), but every line of text in them is a real behaviour of the build.

## Two claims deliberately left out

- **No "₹50,000 asset grant."** The written script says it is in the real output; it is not
  on the captured plan screen. It stays out until someone points at where the build shows it.
- **No "works offline."** The kiosk card says *keeps working when the link drops*, which is
  the honest version: the interview and the record are local, the microphone is not.

# Section 1 explainer — "The problem"

A 41.3-second cut of Speaker 1, `0.0s → 41.3s` of the shot video, ending one frame
before Speaker 2. Same paper and same caption treatment as the other sections, so the
four cuts play back to back without a palette change.

    python3 scripts-build-s1-sources.py   # -> public/s1/*.png + src/s1Sources.ts
    npm run render:s1                     # -> out/explainer_s1.mp4
    npm run render:s1:sfx && npm run normalize:s1:sfx   # effects only

## Every document on screen is the document

`scripts-build-s1-sources.py` renders the crops out of the source PDFs with
`pdftoppm` and reads the **word bounding boxes** out of the same PDFs with
`pdftotext -bbox-layout`. The yellow highlighter is drawn from those boxes, so it
cannot drift off the sentence it is quoting, and re-running the script after a
source is replaced re-cuts both. Nothing on a page is retyped.

| On screen | Source | Claim it carries |
|---|---|---|
| `clause.png` | PM-AJAY Guidelines (revised May 2023), p.23, Ch.3 ¶7A(a)(v)(c) | interest must be assessed before a trade is chosen · the scheme reaches people by *advertisement in print media, social media etc.* |
| `mandate.png` | same, p.24 ¶(vi)(b)(ii) | placement *should by 70% in wage/self-employment* |
| `placement.png` | CAG Report No. 20 of 2025, ¶3.6 | 56.14 lakh certified in 724 job-roles · 41 per cent placed |
| `concentration.png` | CAG Report No. 20 of 2025, ¶2.1.2 | 40 per cent of certifications in only 10 job-roles |
| `tableb.png` | CAG Report No. 20 of 2025, Table 2.1(b) | Green Jobs → Safai Karmchari, 90.35% |
| `cagsite.png` | cag.gov.in audit-report page, captured at 2× | the report exists, tabled 18 Dec 2025 |
| `pmajay.png` · `mosje.png` | socialjustice.gov.in | the scheme logo and the ministry plate |

Both PDFs are in `docs/references/`; the fetched images are in
`docs/Utsav/video/assets/s1/` and the build copies them through. The bar list of ten
job-roles and the five-sector table in `s1Timeline.ts` are typed from Table 2.1(a)
and 2.1(b) and are the only numbers in the section that are not shown as a crop.

## Sound

The soft set only, and quieter than the other sections (0.24–0.34). The delivery note
on this section is *cold and factual, no editorialising* — a sharp transition sting
would do the editorialising the note forbids. No cue lands on "Safai Karmchari"
except one low chime as the name settles.

## What is deliberately not claimed

- **"We found"** appears nowhere. Every number is attributed on screen to the CAG
  report or to the guidelines, which is what Section 5 then builds on.
- **PMKVY is not named on camera**, per the script note; the citation cards carry the
  attribution instead.
- The caption at 13.3s restores one word — **"lakh"** — that the burned-in caption
  drops between "fifty-six" and "people were certified".

`scripts-build-s5-sources.py` is the S1 crop pipeline pointed at the S5 pages: `pdftoppm`
for the pixels, `pdftotext -bbox-layout` for the word boxes, so a highlight cannot drift
off the sentence it quotes. `SourceShot` now merges both manifests, which is why S5 can
reuse the S1 `clause` crop rather than re-cutting the same page.

## Two admissions are on camera, not in a footnote

- **The dialect frame carries the rebuttal to its own claim**: models for all four *do*
  exist, and Bhashini's ASR list carries Bhojpuri and Chhattisgarhi. The claim is the
  error rate, never the absence — saying otherwise in front of this jury would be false.
- **The error-rate table says whose numbers they are**: ours, 38 synthetic Hindi answers
  over a simulated phone line, not dialect audio. `05-measurements.md` says so and so
  does the frame.

## Captions

Read off the burned-in captions at 1 fps, then at 4 fps across every gap the first pass
showed — which is how *"always be correct"*, *"wrong."* and *"Thank you."* were recovered;
all three are missing from a one-second sampling. The delivered grammar is left as
delivered. **One word is restored:** the auto-caption hears *"the NSQF gadget"*; the word
is **gazette**, and that gazette is on screen on the same frame.
