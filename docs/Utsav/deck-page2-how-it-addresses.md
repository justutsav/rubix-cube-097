### 2 · CONVERSATIONAL BENEFICIARY ASSESSMENT
*(heading unchanged)*

### 3 · AI SKILL-GAP ANALYSIS & NSQF PATHWAY MAPPING
*(steps 3 and 4 merged — keywords **AI skill-gap**, **suitability**, **NSQF-aligned**,
**pathway**, **opportunity** all retained)*

> The system **analyses collected information** to **identify skill gaps** and maps the
> beneficiary to suitable **NSQF-aligned training**, **livelihood pathways** and **relevant
> opportunities** `[+]` — each answer classified over a closed set, so a wrong transcript in a
> dialect still yields a right field, and gazetted entry criteria applied as a hard gate before
> ranking. Where she does not qualify: a **NEAR-MISS with the exact gap**, not a silent
> rejection.

`[+]` two things the page is missing. The closed-set classification is the dialect claim stated
safely — claim the method, never the fluency (published WER 27.8 Bhojpuri / 27.4 Chhattisgarhi /
30.4 Magahi / 41.8 Rajasthani, ARTPARK-IISc). The NEAR-MISS gate is our strongest
differentiator, run over the 2,814-row NQR register, sha256-pinned.

## Which variant

- **Main version** (merge 3+4): keeps the *access → interview* distinction, which is the half
  a non-technical judge understands fastest. Cost: circle 3 carries two ideas.
- **Variant B** (merge 1+2): gives the **NEAR-MISS eligibility gate** its own circle — the one
  thing on this page no other team can claim. Cost: circle 1 carries two ideas, and it is the
  circle everyone reads first.

Recommend **Variant B**: circle 1 merges two things the beneficiary experiences as *one phone
call*, so the merge is invisible to the reader, whereas merging analysis with NSQF mapping
hides the differentiator inside a dense box.
